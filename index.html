What Is Considered a Dental Emergency?

Not every dental problem needs immediate attention, but some situations cannot wait. You should seek emergency oral care if you experience:

  • Severe or throbbing tooth pain that does not ease with over-the-counter medicine
  • Swelling in the gums, cheek or face
  • A knocked-out or badly loosened adult tooth
  • A cracked, broken or chipped tooth with pain or sharp edges
  • A pimple-like bump on the gums that may contain pus
  • Bleeding that will not stop after an injury to the mouth
  • A lost crown or filling that leaves the tooth exposed

When you are unsure, it is safest to call. A short conversation with our team can help you decide whether you need a same day dentist visit or can safely wait for a regular appointment.

Toothache Relief and Dental Pain Relief

A toothache is your body's way of saying something is wrong. The cause may be deep decay, a cracked tooth, gum disease, an infected nerve or a developing abscess. Pain medicine may dull the discomfort for a few hours, but it does not fix the cause. During an emergency dental visit, we examine the tooth, take digital X-rays when needed and explain what is happening in plain language. Our priority is toothache relief first, so you feel better before we plan any follow-up care.

Until you can see us, you can rinse with warm salt water, gently floss to remove trapped food, apply a cold compress to the outside of the cheek and take over-the-counter pain relief as directed on the label. Avoid very hot, cold or sugary foods, and never place aspirin directly against the gum because it can burn the tissue.

Dental Abscess Treatment

A dental abscess is a pocket of pus caused by a bacterial infection at the root of a tooth or in the gums. Signs include intense pain, swelling, a bad taste in the mouth, fever and tenderness when you chew. An abscess does not heal on its own. Without dental abscess treatment, the infection can spread into the jaw, neck or bloodstream. Treatment may involve draining the abscess, prescribing antibiotics, performing a root canal or removing the tooth if it cannot be saved. If you have facial swelling together with a fever, or trouble breathing or swallowing, go to the nearest emergency room immediately.

Emergency Root Canal Treatment

Many people fear root canals, but the procedure is designed to relieve pain, not cause it. An emergency root canal removes the infected or inflamed pulp inside your tooth, cleans the canals and seals them to prevent reinfection. With modern anesthesia, most patients describe the experience as similar to having a filling placed. Saving your natural tooth protects your bite, keeps neighboring teeth stable and often costs less over time than replacing a missing tooth.

Emergency Tooth Extraction

Sometimes a tooth is simply too damaged, decayed or infected to be restored. In that case, an emergency tooth extraction is the safest way to end the pain and protect your health. We use effective numbing and gentle technique, and we provide clear aftercare instructions covering bleeding, eating, medication and healing. Once the area has healed, we can discuss replacement options such as bridges or implants so you can restore your smile and your ability to chew comfortably.

Broken Tooth Repair and Chipped Tooth Fix

Biting down on ice, nuts or hard candy, a fall, or a sports injury can crack or chip a tooth. A tiny chip may look like a cosmetic issue, but it can create sharp edges that cut the tongue and cheek and can allow bacteria to reach the sensitive inner tooth. Deeper fractures can expose the nerve and cause serious pain.

Our broken tooth repair and chipped tooth fix options may include dental bonding, a filling, a crown or a veneer, depending on the size and location of the damage. If you can, save any broken pieces, rinse your mouth with warm water, and cover sharp edges with sugar-free gum or dental wax until you reach our office.

Dental Trauma Repair

Accidents happen at home, at work and on the sports field. Dental trauma repair addresses injuries such as knocked-out teeth, displaced teeth, fractured roots and soft tissue injuries of the lips, gums and cheeks. Time matters most when a permanent tooth is knocked out. Pick it up by the crown, never the root. Gently rinse it with milk or water without scrubbing, and try to place it back into the socket. If that is not possible, store it in a cup of milk and call 240-224-8082 right away. A tooth that is replanted within about an hour has the best chance of survival.

Why Delaying Emergency Dental Treatment Is Risky

It is tempting to hope a toothache will go away. Sometimes the pain does fade, but that can mean the nerve inside the tooth has died while the infection continues to spread. Waiting often leads to:

  • More complex and more expensive treatment later
  • Loss of a tooth that could have been saved
  • Infection spreading to the jaw, face or neck
  • Damage to nearby teeth and bone
  • Poor sleep, difficulty eating and missed work or school

Choosing an affordable emergency dentist early is one of the best ways to control both pain and cost. A small problem treated promptly is nearly always simpler than a major one treated late.

Tips to Prevent Dental Emergencies

  • Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste and floss every day.
  • Schedule regular checkups and cleanings so problems are caught early.
  • Wear a mouthguard for contact sports and a night guard if you grind your teeth.
  • Avoid chewing ice, pen caps and hard candy, and never open packages with your teeth.
  • Do not ignore minor sensitivity, small chips or bleeding gums.
